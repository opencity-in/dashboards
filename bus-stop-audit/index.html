State of Bus Stops

This dashboard shares findings from the first phase of a citizen audit of bus stops that mobilised residents to assess stop conditions and usability. The project aims to foster community ownership and provide evidence-based recommendations for more accessible, user-friendly public transport amenities.

Where to Act First: Priority Map

Every stop starts at 100 and loses 10 marks for each of the 10 problems we checked for: no shelter, inadequate roof, poor lighting, unsafe at night, heavy encroachment, blocked pedestrian path, no ramp, bus stopping more than a metre out, overcrowding, waterlogging. A lower score means more problems were found. Click any marker to see that stop's issues.

Five Scorecards for Every Stop

Every stop is scored out of 100 on five things. Each scorecard is marked out of its own total — a stop keeps the marks it earns and loses the rest, so a lower score is a worse stop. Bands: Poor 0–25, Weak 26–50, Fair 51–75, Good 76–100. A stop with no shelter loses all the shelter marks — no shelter means no roof, no seat and no route board. Switch tabs to recolour the map; the list shows the lowest scoring stops for the scorecard you pick.

How each score is worked out

Each scorecard carries its own number of marks — Shade & Heat 7, Shelter 10, Access 11, Safety 10, Information 6. A stop loses marks for each problem found, and its score is the share of marks it kept, shown out of 100.

Worked example — Adyar Depot, Shade & Heat Protection. That scorecard is worth 7 marks. The stop has no shelter, so it loses 3 marks for no shade and 2 for no roof; it also has no drinking water, losing 1 more. That is 6 marks gone. It keeps 1 of 7, which shows as 14 out of 100.

The priority map at the top of the page works the same way, but its sums are simpler: 10 problems, each worth 10 marks, so a stop with 3 problems scores 70.

Some problems take marks off two scorecards because they cause two kinds of harm — a wide boarding gap counts against both Access and Safety. Where a stop was only visited in daytime, Safety is marked out of 7 instead of 10, so those stops are not penalised for a night rating nobody could give.

Accessibility & Pedestrian Safety

The audit checked whether stops can be reached and used by everyone — including wheelchair users, the visually impaired, and the elderly — and whether the stop itself obstructs footpath movement.

Access features present (% of stops)

Surveyors ticked every feature they saw, so these do not add up to 100%.

Pedestrian movement around the shelter

At 3 in 4 stops, the shelter blocks the footpath — pedestrians and wheelchair users must step into live traffic to get past.

Physical usability of the stop area
Only 34 stops (18%) allow level, flush boarding. At 43 stops buses halt more than a metre into the carriageway, so passengers walk through moving traffic to board.

Lighting and safety ratings move together

Lighting at stops rated "Unsafe" vs "Safe"
Of 32 stops rated unsafe, 20 (63%) have no adequate lighting. Of 75 safe-rated stops, only 4 lack lighting. Stops that feel unsafe are overwhelmingly the unlit ones.

Weather protection gap: among sheltered stops, 49% have a roof that is inadequate (too tall, too small, gaps) or too damaged to keep rain off. 42% of stops are overcrowded, with waiting passengers standing on the road. Amenities beyond a bench are near-absent: drinking water at 9 stops, public toilet visible from 2, CCTV at 3.

Take This Audit to Your City

This audit was carried out entirely by citizen volunteers using a free, open-source toolkit — and it can be repeated in any city or neighbourhood. If you are a resident group, civic organisation, student collective or government agency interested in running a similar bus stop audit where you live, we would be glad to help.
